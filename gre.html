How this tracker works (the method)
The GRE General Test is Verbal Reasoning (130–170), Quantitative Reasoning (130–170) and one Analytical Writing essay (0–6), about 2 hours in all since the shorter format began in September 2023. Prep providers generally suggest 8–12 weeks of steady work for someone starting cold, with plenty of timed practice. This follows that shape.
  1. Diagnostic first. Take one full timed practice test before studying anything. It shows your starting score and which topics to push on.
  2. Learn. Work through the topic: concepts, then worked examples. The schedule is built around this step.
  3. Drill. Do 10–20 questions on it the same day or the next and enter your % correct (for essays, your self-score out of 6). 70%+ is on track, 60–69 needs a second look, and <60 is a weak area that gets priority in final review.
  4. Recall checks. 7 days after a drill, and again 3 weeks later, the chapter shows up under Recall checks. Spend 10 minutes redoing a few questions or testing yourself from memory. This matters most for vocabulary, which fades fast.
  5. Topic test. When a whole topic is finished, do 20–30 mixed questions and enter the score at the bottom of that topic.
  6. Final review & mocks. The last days before the test are kept for full timed mocks, debriefs and logistics. Enter each mock's total score (260–340); it is colored against your target.
Busy period. The planner treats the days you mark as busy as light days (default 40% of a normal day, so roughly a night session), and spreads the chapters across the remaining capacity. Change the dates or load, then press Re-plan remaining. Readiness is your drill scores weighted by each topic's share of the score.
Retake rule and score timing per ETS: one attempt every 21 days, up to five in 12 months; official scores in about 8–10 days. Sources: ETS: scheduling & retakes · ETS information bulletin
